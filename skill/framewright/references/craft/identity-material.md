# Identity, reference roles, and local revision — Framewright craft reference

Use with Core §§8.1, 8.5.1, 8.6, and 9 when identity, asymmetric anatomy, mixed reference roles, or a fragile local revision is material. This reference works within the existing Material Registry and Intent Ledger. It does not create a second character card, identity database, or image-editing workflow.

## Resolve ownership at the property that matters

Separate persistent identity from current pose, wardrobe state, camera angle, location, light, and style. A selected generated frame can show the accepted transient state without replacing canonical identity. A face reference need not control a mechanical torso; a spatial Plate need not control the final character; a motion donor need not contribute its actor or room.

For asymmetric subjects, write anatomical left/right explicitly where needed and separately state screen placement. Confirm which side is visible after a turn. For a cropped limb or close-up, name its owner, the relevant structure, contact point, and relative scale. Do not infer an unseen side by mirroring a visible side unless the source actually establishes symmetry.

When references disagree, resolve the property conflict using the approved sources rather than averaging them. Exclude unrelated details from transfer. More references are not automatically better: follow Core's runtime admission decision and the selected adapter's actual capabilities.

## Keep opening state distinct from an intermediate example

An image showing a later pose or open object does not become a first frame because it was uploaded first. Establish whether it is the opening boundary, an intermediate state, an endpoint, or a source of particular properties. Only use a runtime role supported by the selected adapter; do not invent model-specific binding syntax.

Distinguish what is physically in the image from what the next generation should do with it. The director may want the geometry while rejecting the pose, or the lighting while rejecting the person. If the surface cannot separate those properties reliably, explain the conditioning risk and propose text extraction, cropping, a different source, or an authorized different route.

## Revise the failure while protecting the success

Identify the region or relationship being changed and the already approved features that must survive: silhouette, proportion, composition, expression, surface character, depth, or light. Preserve these through the current adapter's editing contract. A candidate being liked or accepted does not by itself grant a new editing baseline; follow the explicit baseline/reset policy.

Do not interpret every irregularity as contamination. Distinguish:

- structural detail: seams, joints, anatomy, silhouette, designed linework;
- intentional surface history: wear, moisture, patina, grain, brushwork;
- optical or environmental appearance: defocus, flare, haze, reflections, atmosphere;
- unintended artifacts: unsupported pseudo-grooves, incoherent dirt, duplicated parts, broken edges;
- uncertain features that need inspection or a material clarification.

This distinction protects the approved look. It does not authorize removing all texture, adding plausible-looking machinery, or making every material new and smooth. Inspect relevant details and the whole frame: a cleaner knee is not a successful edit if the other leg, shoulder, composition, or atmosphere was damaged.

## Optional specialist handoff

The checks above are native to Framewright and do not require another installed skill. If the user separately selects Image Master or another image specialist for an actual repair, hand over the existing approved goal, source roles, locked features, change region, baseline, and authorized action. Do not rerun director intake or create duplicate editable intent records. Specialist output is an artifact or proposal for review; it cannot silently change the shot, identity authority, aesthetic locks, or acceptance status.

Distinguish deterministic preservation from generative preservation. Describe masks, layers, reference weights, or hard locks only when the selected tool actually provides them. When control is soft, identify likely nearby drift instead of claiming pixel identity. Rendering and paid retries still require the authorization applicable to the task.
